# Coverage Bound and Prior ESS Experiments

## Questions

This experiment checks the claims introduced by
`coverage_hoeffding_derivation.md` and `MTM08_analysis.md`:

1. Does dataset-level greedy selection attain its finite-budget coverage guarantee?
2. Under the stated Lipschitz and conditional-independence assumptions, do the
   target-matched and uniform prior-accuracy bounds cover their errors at the
   nominal 95 percent level?
3. How different are concentration ESS, added Beta prior strength, and total
   MTM08 Beta ESS in practice?
4. Does the similarity-weighted estimator necessarily beat uniform calibration?
5. On saved real Text-to-SQL runs, how sensitive is the collision-aware estimator
   to the power-prior discount and to item- versus model-item-scaled beta strength?

The implementation is in `pooleval/prior_bounds.py`; the runner is
`experiments/run_prior_bound_validation.py`; focused tests are in
`tests/test_prior_bounds.py`. Raw results are written to
`results/prior_bound_validation.json`.

## Executive Verdict

> **Overall answer: the new theory works as a conditional bound, but it is not
> validated as a real-data accuracy guarantee.**

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Beta ESS is the sum of Beta shape parameters | **PASS** | `Beta(3,7)` returns ESS `10`; the power prior returns `s + 2`; unit tests pass. |
| Greedy dataset coverage has the stated finite-budget guarantee | **PASS** | 500/500 controlled trials satisfied the exact `K=3` guarantee; mean greedy/optimal coverage was `0.9988`. |
| Coverage plus matching ESS gives a valid accuracy bound | **PASS, CONDITIONAL** | Both bounds covered 100% of controlled model-level errors, above nominal 95%, under enforced Lipschitz and independence assumptions. |
| The bound is validated on real Text-to-SQL targets | **NOT ESTABLISHED** | Existing artifacts lack the source/target embeddings and per-item source outcomes needed for the transfer term. |
| Similarity-weighted matching always improves point accuracy | **REFUTED AS A GENERAL CLAIM** | Matching MAE was `6.38` points versus `4.08` for uniform averaging in the controlled trial. |
| Full historical discount is best for these real artifacts | **SUPPORTED, DESCRIPTIVE** | `a0=1` had mean MAE `11.25`, versus `31.59` at `a0=0`, across four targets. |
| Model-item beta scaling is justified as extra independent information | **NOT SUPPORTED** | Strength `1200` did not improve aggregate MAE or Kendall over item strength `120`; fitted beta shifted from `0.705` to `0.611`. |

Therefore: **the derivations and implementation are internally correct, and the
conditional theorem behaves as predicted; the experiments do not show that its
assumptions hold for real Text-to-SQL data or that the similarity-derived discount
improves accuracy.**

## Bound-Theory Results

These are the direct tests of the Hoeffding and monotone-submodular claims. They are
not MAE rows because a bound is a certificate on error, not a point prediction.

| Theory claim | Measured result | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| Monotonicity: adding a dataset never lowers coverage | 500/500 trials | **PASS** |
| Greedy submodular guarantee, `K=3` | 500/500 trials; greedy/optimal coverage `0.9988 +/- 0.0001` | **PASS** |
| Finite-budget lower bound `1-(1-1/K)^K` | 500/500 trials satisfied it | **PASS** |
| Weighted Hoeffding bound, matched weights | Empirical coverage `100.0%` at nominal `95%` | **PASS, conservative** |
| Uniform-prior three-term bound | Empirical coverage `100.0%` at nominal `95%` | **PASS, conservative** |
| Mean matched bound | `0.2936 +/- 0.0021` absolute accuracy error | Useful in controlled test |
| Mean uniform-prior bound | `0.6697 +/- 0.0041` absolute accuracy error | Valid but looser |
| Real Text2SQL/Image/Node bound validation | No source-target embeddings or assignments in artifacts | **NOT TESTED** |

The controlled experiment therefore supports the mathematical result **under its
assumptions**: input-only greedy selection preserved the guarantee, and independent
bounded calibration outcomes obeyed the Hoeffding certificate. It does **not** prove
that real model correctness is Lipschitz in the chosen embedding or that real
calibration outcomes are conditionally independent. Those assumptions are the
remaining threat to applying the bound to Text2SQL, image classification, or node
classification.

### What "Add the Two Errors" Means

For the target-matched estimator, the combined result is exactly:

`absolute error <= transfer error + Hoeffding sampling error`

In the controlled run, the mean decomposition was:

| Term | Mean absolute-error allowance |
|---|---:|
| Coverage transfer term `2 L sqrt(1 - coverage)` | **0.0760** |
| Weighted Hoeffding term `sqrt(log(2/delta)/(2 n_eff))` | **0.2175** |
| Combined bound | **0.2936** |

The observed matched-prior MAE was **0.0638**, below the combined bound, and the
combined bound covered **100%** of model-level errors in the 500-trial experiment.
The two terms are added by the triangle inequality; they are not two probabilities,
so the confidence remains `1 - delta = 0.95`, not `0.90`. For the current uniform
prior, the markdown correctly adds a third deterministic term for weight mismatch:

`uniform bound = uniform Hoeffding error + weight mismatch + transfer error`.

## Protocol

### Controlled theorem check

We ran 500 independently generated problems with six models per problem. Each
problem had 240 held-out target items, eight candidate calibration datasets with
36 items each, and a budget of three datasets. Greedy selection used only shifted
cosine similarities. Exact enumeration over the 56 possible three-dataset subsets
provided the optimal coverage value.

Expected correctness was generated as a linear function of unit embeddings. The
known coefficient norm is therefore a valid Lipschitz constant. Calibration and
target outcomes were independent Bernoulli draws conditional on the inputs. These
choices deliberately satisfy the theorem's assumptions; the experiment checks the
implementation and finite-sample behavior, not whether those assumptions hold for
natural Text-to-SQL errors.

### Held-out real-data ablation

We used the saved clean Spider, SQLFlow, BIRD, and BIRD-MiniDev pool artifacts.
For each target, collision rates and pseudo-label quality were estimated from the
other three labeled datasets. Target gold labels were used only to compute MAE,
Kendall tau, and Top-1 after fitting. Each artifact implies 120 calibration items.

The power discount `a0` was swept over 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1.0. Alpha and beta
used added strength `s = a0 * 120`. At `a0 = 1`, beta strength was additionally
compared at zero, item scale (`120`), and model-item scale (`1200`).

Results are means over 500 trials; `+/-` is a normal 95 percent confidence interval
for the trial mean.

| Diagnostic | Result |
|---|---:|

| Finite-K greedy guarantee satisfied | 100.0% |
| Normalized achieved coverage | 0.9500 +/- 0.0002 |
| Selected calibration count | 108.0 |
| Matching concentration ESS | 40.22 +/- 0.67 |
| Uniform/matching total-variation mismatch | 0.4629 +/- 0.0040 |
| Matched prior MAE to expected target accuracy | 6.38 +/- 0.17 points |
| Uniform prior MAE to expected target accuracy | 4.08 +/- 0.11 points |
| Matched 95% bound empirical coverage | 100.0% |
| Uniform 95% bound empirical coverage | 100.0% |
| Mean matched bound | 0.2936 +/- 0.0021 |
| Mean uniform bound | 0.6697 +/- 0.0041 |
| Matched bounds no larger than one | 100.0% |
| Mean similarity discount `a0` | 0.9500 +/- 0.0002 |
| Mean added Beta strength `s` | 102.60 +/- 0.02 |
| Mean total MTM08 Beta ESS `s + 2` | 104.60 +/- 0.02 |
| Mean MAP prior weight `s / (N + s)` | 0.2995 |

Every deterministic transfer inequality also held. Both stochastic bounds exceeded
their nominal 95 percent coverage, so this run found no violation. Their 100 percent
empirical coverage indicates conservatism, not a stronger confidence guarantee.

Target matching did not minimize MAE in this data-generating process. Its assignment
weights concentrated 108 observations into an ESS of about 40, raising sampling
variance. Uniform averaging had lower realized MAE here even though
its worst-case theorem bound was more than twice as wide. This confirms that the
new theory is a validity bound, not a claim that matching always improves point
estimation. The exact 90/10 perfect-coverage counterexample from the derivation is
also encoded as a unit test and recovers weights `(0.9, 0.1)`, matched accuracy
`0.82`, uniform accuracy `0.50`, and mismatch bound `0.40`.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.00 | 0 | 2 | 31.59 | 0.561 | 0.00 | 0.834 |

Full historical strength was best on aggregate for both MAE and Kendall in this
sweep. This does not establish that `a0 = 1` is universally optimal: all four runs
use source priors based on closely related benchmark data, and the artifacts do not
contain the source-target embeddings needed to evaluate the paper's proposed
similarity-based `a0` on real data.

## Real Beta-Scaling Ablation

Alpha strength is fixed at 120 in all rows.

| Beta scaling | Beta strength | MAE points | Kendall | Top-1 | Fitted beta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No beta anchor | 0 | 11.11 | 0.536 | 0.25 | 0.750 |
| Item-scaled | 120 | 11.25 | 0.674 | 0.25 | 0.705 |
| Model-item-scaled | 1200 | 11.16 | 0.649 | 0.25 | 0.611 |

Multiplying beta strength by the ten-model pool size did not improve aggregate MAE,
Kendall, or Top-1 over item scaling. It also moved fitted beta materially, from
0.705 to 0.611. Effects were heterogeneous: pair scaling helped SQLFlow and BIRD
MAE but hurt Spider and BIRD-MiniDev. The experiment therefore provides no support
for interpreting the factor `J` as independent calibration information. Item
scaling is the more defensible default unless a joint dependence model justifies
the larger strength.

## Final Conclusion

**Confirmed:** the Beta ESS algebra, greedy coverage guarantee, coverage-to-distance
transfer calculation, and weighted Hoeffding calculation agree with the new notes
and pass implementation tests. Under the controlled assumptions, the bounds covered
every observed error.

**Not confirmed:** this does not establish that embedding similarity is a valid
correctness-smoothness proxy for real Text-to-SQL, or that the similarity-derived
discount is optimal. The controlled point-estimation comparison actually favored
uniform averaging because matching concentrated the weights and reduced concentration
ESS.

**Recommendation:** report added strength `s`, total MTM08 ESS `s + 2`, and matching
ESS `1 / sum(w^2)` as separate quantities. Retain the uniform-weight mismatch term
when using the current estimator, and do not multiply beta strength by model count
without a dependence model.

The remaining empirical gap is a real-data test of the transfer bound itself. That
requires storing source and target item embeddings, selected-dataset membership,
per-item source correctness, and input-only matching assignments. The current saved
pool artifacts contain aggregate priors and target outputs but not those source-side
quantities, so calculating a real bound from them would require fabricating data.
